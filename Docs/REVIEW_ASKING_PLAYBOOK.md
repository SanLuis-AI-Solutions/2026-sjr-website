# Review Asking Playbook

Updated: 2026-07-02

## Goal

Increase consistent Google review requests without adding paid software or slowing down the repair counter.

## When To Ask

Ask after a customer shows a clear positive signal:

- They say the piece looks good.
- They thank the shop.
- They pick up a completed repair without an issue.
- They mention they will come back or refer someone.

Do not ask while a customer is upset, waiting on an unresolved issue, or still deciding whether to approve work.

## Counter Script

Use this short version:

> If you are happy with the repair, a Google review would really help our family shop. There is a QR code right here, and it only takes a minute.

Use this warmer version for longtime or very happy customers:

> We are trying to help more local customers find us for in-house jewelry and watch repair. If you are happy with the work, would you mind leaving us a quick Google review using this QR code?

## Staff Habit

At pickup, use a simple three-step habit:

1. Confirm the customer is happy with the repair.
2. Ask for the review before they walk away.
3. Point directly to the printed QR sign.

## Daily Target

Minimum target:

- Ask 5 satisfied customers per open day.

Stretch target:

- Ask every clearly satisfied pickup customer.

## Tracking

Keep this simple. At the end of each day, write down:

- Review asks made
- Customers who said yes
- New Google reviews noticed
- Any objections customers gave

## Objections

If they say they do not have time:

> No problem at all. The QR code is here whenever it is convenient.

If they are unsure what to write:

> Just one sentence about what we repaired and how it went is perfect.

If they do not use Google:

> No worries. We appreciate you coming in.

## Weekly Check

Every Monday, compare:

- New Google reviews
- Average rating
- Review clicks from GA4 if available
- GBP calls/direction activity if available
